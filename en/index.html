THE NOVEL

Something has remained buried in the ice.

Deep beneath the Svalbard permafrost, a bacterium is discovered with the ability to consume plastic.

It could be the answer to one of the planet's greatest environmental crises. But when microbiologist Elena Dal Bon enters the laboratories of HelixCrest, she realizes that someone has already understood the discovery's true potential.

Unexplained deaths. Hidden experiments. A project that is no longer just about saving the environment.

Behind the promise of saving the world, something far more dangerous is taking shape: an organism that could become an instrument of global control.

And when the threat reaches her son, Elena is forced to make an impossible choice.

Save the world.
Or save him.

THE BOOK

The perfect solution can become the perfect weapon.

PLASTOPHAGE is a scientific techno-thriller about biotechnology, environmental crisis and ideological obsession.

READ THE OPENING

Chapter 1 — Cavity

Drilling camp, Aurora Nord Base, Svalbard · 79°08′N 17°25′E — February 17, 2025, 14:32 UTC

The W-TRX 300 vibracore drill had been working the compact ice for an hour.

At seventy-nine degrees north latitude, in mid-February, the sun still hadn’t made up its mind to return. There was a low, indirect light for a few hours a day; the rest was a blue dusk that never became real darkness or real morning.

Outside the containers the temperature hovered around minus twenty-eight, and the wind drove it lower.

It wasn’t a place you came to by chance.

Reaching Aurora Nord Base took two flights, a snowmobile, and a permit that HelixCrest SA had obtained by presenting the project as research into the composition of ancient permafrost.

That was true, in part.

Like almost everything to do with Stein, it was true in part.

THE AUTHOR

Jason Barkley

He writes stories about the exact point where science, power and ambition stop being controllable.

Jason Barkley is the pseudonym behind PLASTOPHAGE, a biotech thriller exploring the boundary between scientific progress and unpredictable consequences.

In his stories, technology is neither good nor evil. People decide what to do with it.

PLASTOPHAGE began with a question:

What if the solution to one of the planet's greatest problems became an even bigger problem?

Jason Barkley chooses to let the stories speak instead of his biography.
